*[Previously,]({% link sept-4.md %}) the system fulfilled a real bill of quantities and cleared a known blocker to reach a target underneath. This week had almost none of that — it was desk work, start to finish.*

## Writing the paper

Most of this week was spent in front of a screen that wasn't connected to a robot. The ~8-page IEEE-format research article needed every result from the last four weeks. Tables, figures, captions, references — the kind of work that doesn't photograph well and doesn't make for exciting blog content.

The structure roughly followed the four research questions: perception first, then sequencing, verification, and the isolated-vs-crowded deficit that ties them together. Getting the discussion section right — specifically, being honest about the 57% crowded figure without burying the 93% isolated one — took more drafts than I'd like to admit.

> Since this was almost entirely a desk-work week, there isn't much media to put here. No bench photos, no gripper overlays, no depth maps — just a lot of LaTeX and a lot of coffee.

## Saturday — one last lab visit

Went back to the lab on Saturday the 26th for a final rehearsal. The goal was: run through the full pipeline end-to-end one more time to make sure nothing had drifted since the last real session, and capture the **demo video** — the one that now sits at the top of this blog's [home page]({% link index.md %}).

Setting up the bench, loading the BOQ, running the system, filming it — the whole thing took a couple of hours. The arm cooperated, the gripper cooperated, and the camera cooperated, which, if you've been reading these entries, you'll know is not a guarantee.

## Where this leaves me

Report submitted on the 25th. Demo video in the bag. One thing left: the presentation itself.
